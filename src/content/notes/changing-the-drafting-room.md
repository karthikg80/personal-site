# Changing the Drafting Room

I’ve been changing the Drafting Room on my site.

The first version was built around a fairly obvious idea: give an AI some notes, have a conversation, and eventually get better writing out of it.

That increasingly felt like the wrong goal.

I don’t particularly need another place where I can ask an AI to “make this better.” There are already plenty of those.

What I want is something closer to an editor sitting beside me while I work.

An editor who notices when there is something interesting buried in a paragraph. Who asks about the contradiction I casually skipped past. Who points out when a sentence sounds like me — and when I suddenly sound like I’m performing the role of Someone Who Writes On The Internet.

And importantly, one who doesn’t quietly fill the gaps.

So I changed the rules.

The Drafting Room is now explicitly constrained to the material I give it. It shouldn’t invent an experience, an opinion, a fact, certainty I don’t have, or a satisfying conclusion just because essays are supposed to have one.

It also shouldn’t turn my writing into an imitation of another writer.

There is a mode now that reflects on voice: what feels natural, what might be performed, and something worth experimenting with in the next revision.

I don’t think “my voice” is a style guide that can be discovered and frozen. I’d rather let it emerge from repeated writing — odd phrases, fragments, jokes, recurring obsessions, contradictions and all.

The other boundary is between private writing and published writing.

The Drafting Room is private. Details about work, family, places or other people can sometimes be exactly what makes a thought concrete.

But concrete doesn’t mean publishable.

The system can flag sensitive material, but it cannot declare something safe to publish. A draft leaving the room still lands as a draft. Publishing remains a separate human decision.

The more capable these systems become, the easier it is to collapse thinking → writing → editing → approving → publishing into one button.

I’m deliberately moving in the other direction.

The Drafting Room can help me think and challenge the writing. But it doesn’t get to decide what I believe, what my voice is, or what belongs on the public web.
